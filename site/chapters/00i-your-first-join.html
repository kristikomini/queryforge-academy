Why the data is in pieces

🧒 Simple

The order cards do not have the customer's name written on them. They have the customer's number. That way, if the customer changes their name, you correct one customer card instead of hunting through a thousand order cards — and you cannot end up with two different spellings. A join is the moment you put an order card next to its customer card so you can read both at once.

🎓 Professional

Storing the customer's name on every order would store one fact many times, which creates an update anomaly: correcting it means finding every copy, and missing one leaves the database holding two answers to the same question. So the order stores a foreign key — a pointer — and a join reassembles the two at read time. That trade is the substance of chapter 05, and the reason joins are the most-used operation in SQL.

The mistake everybody makes once

More than two tables

⚠ Forgetting the ON clause

A join with no match rule is a cross join: every row of A paired with every row of B. With 1800 orders and 300 customers that is 540 000 rows, and the query looks like it has hung. Some engines refuse a bare JOIN without ON; others quietly obey. If a result is enormous and every value looks repeated, count the JOINs and count the ONs — they should match.

  1. Data is split across tables so that each fact is stored once; a join reassembles it at read time.
  2. JOIN … ON pairs rows by a match rule, almost always foreign key = primary key.
  3. INNER JOIN keeps only matched pairs; LEFT JOIN keeps every left row and pads the right side with NULL.
  4. A condition in ON is part of the match; the same condition in WHERE filters after the padding, and silently turns a LEFT JOIN into an inner one.
  5. Alias every table and qualify every column: with two tables it is politeness, with four it is the difference between readable and not.
  6. A JOIN with no ON is a cross product — count your joins against your match rules.

What is the difference between INNER JOIN and LEFT JOIN?

An inner join returns only rows that matched on both sides. A left join returns every row of the left table, with the right-hand columns filled with NULL where nothing matched. Pick by the question: “customers and their orders, if any” needs the left join; “orders with their customer” is inner.

A LEFT JOIN report stopped showing customers with no orders after somebody added a date filter. Why?

The filter was put in WHERE instead of ON. Padded rows carry NULL in the right-hand columns, the comparison against NULL is UNKNOWN, and WHERE discards them — so the outer join became an inner join with no error. Move the condition into the ON clause.
