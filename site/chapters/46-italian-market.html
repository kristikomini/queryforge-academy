The Italian market: gestionali, ERP data and BI

What an Emilia-Romagna software house actually asks a database developer to do on a Tuesday morning.

Who is actually hiring

🧒 Simple

Most of the companies with SQL jobs in Italy do not sell software to the public. They sell software to other companies — the system that runs a factory, a warehouse, a wholesaler, an accountant's office — and those systems are twenty years old, full of data, and absolutely cannot stop working.

In Emilia-Romagna specifically, the first two dominate: packaging machinery, ceramics, food processing, automotive components and the software houses that serve them. Bologna, Modena and Reggio Emilia are the density.

What you actually do on a Tuesday

🎓 The real distribution of work
  1. “This report is wrong.” Reconcile a number against the source, find the join that fans out or the filter that excludes cancelled orders in one query and not the other. This is the single most common ticket, and chapter 48 is how to turn it into a specification.
  2. “This is slow.” A screen, a report or a nightly job. Parts 4 and chapter 42.
  3. A new report or extract for a client, or for the finance department.
  4. An integration: data in or out of another system, by file, database link or web service.
  5. A schema change to support a feature — and making it safe on a customer's database you cannot see.
The constraint that shapes everything: multi-tenancy by installation

A gestionale is installed at each customer, often on their own server, sometimes versions behind. So your schema change must apply to forty databases you have never seen, whose data volumes differ by three orders of magnitude, some of which have local customisations. That is why versioned migrations and backward-compatible changes matter so much more here than in a single-tenant SaaS — and demonstrating that you understand it is unusual and valuable.

Fatturazione elettronica and the SDI

🎓 The Italy-specific thing worth knowing exists

Since 2019, essentially all invoices between Italian businesses must be issued as FatturaPA XML and routed through the Sistema di Interscambio (SDI), the Revenue Agency's clearing house. It is a state-run message bus that every business system in the country must speak, and it lands on the database in specific ways:

  • Invoice numbering must be gapless and sequential per year. That is a legal requirement, and it is a genuinely hard concurrency problem — an IDENTITY column leaves gaps whenever a transaction rolls back (chapter 24).
  • Every invoice has a lifecycle of SDI outcomes — accepted, rejected, not delivered, each with its own timestamp and receipt. That is a state machine and an event log, exactly like the reference database's orders.
  • Rejections must be re-sendable, which means idempotency keys and a queue (chapter 20).
  • Ten-year retention (conservazione sostitutiva) — which collides with GDPR erasure, and is resolved by pseudonymisation (chapter 35).
✔ Why the gapless-number problem is worth having an answer to

An auto-increment leaves gaps, and gaps are not allowed. Serialising on a counter row is correct and becomes a contention point. The usual production answer is to allocate the number at the last possible moment, inside the same transaction as the commit of the invoice, from a per-year counter row locked with SELECT … FOR UPDATE — accepting the serialisation because invoices are not issued thousands per second. Being able to discuss that trade-off is a very strong signal in an interview at an Italian software house, because it is a problem they have definitely argued about.

Reporting: SSRS, Power BI, and the analyst

Most “Power BI” roles at this level are not about DAX. They are about the layer underneath: giving the analyst a model they cannot get wrong. Concretely:

  • Views, not tables. The analyst gets v_sales_by_month, not orders — so the joins, the cancelled-order exclusion and the VAT treatment are decided once, by you, and every report agrees.
  • A star schema, not a flat view. Power BI's engine is columnar and expects one; a report built on a wide flat extract or on the OLTP schema will be slow in ways no DAX fixes (chapter 39).
  • A dim_date with is_holiday, because “sales per working day” is a real request and Ferragosto is not a normal Friday.
  • Import mode over DirectQuery for most reports — DirectQuery sends a query per visual interaction straight at your production database, which is a load pattern nobody plans for.

The language question

Where Italian and English actually sit

Technical vocabulary stays English — la query, il deploy, fare il merge, il database, la performance, il backup. Translating those marks somebody who learned from a textbook. What is genuinely Italian is the process and contract vocabulary: collaudo (acceptance testing), avvio in produzione (go-live), presa in carico (taking ownership of a ticket), RAL (gross annual salary), preventivo (quote), capitolato (specification), ticket aperto. Those are where a technically strong candidate loses an interview, and the glossary and phrasebook exist for exactly that.

⚠ Trap — the false friends that cost you
  • smart working means remote work. It is not a comment on how smart the work is, and using it in the English sense will confuse everybody.
  • Sono confuso is fine; sono confuso/a about a specification is normal to say. But «non ho capito» lands much better than a long hedge — Italian technical conversation is direct, and over-softening reads as evasive.
  • Formal lei in the first interview, and switch to tu when they do — which is usually within ten minutes at a small software house, and later or never in a bank.
  • Un attimo is not a minute. Neither is «ti richiamo subito». Do not build a schedule on either.

What to expect from the process

  • The recruiter call is in Italian, is about availability, notice period and RAL expectations, and is usually not technical at all.
  • A written test is common: three to six SQL exercises, sometimes a take-home. See chapter 50.
  • The technical interview is often with the person you would work for, is conversational rather than a quiz, and frequently includes “here is a slow query, what would you do?”
  • RAL is quoted gross and annual, in 13 or 14 monthly instalments. A junior database developer in Emilia-Romagna is typically in the region of €25–32k RAL, mid €35–45k; consultancies pay less and place you faster. Check current figures — this is the number that dates fastest in this whole course.
  • The CCNL matters. The national contract (usually metalmeccanico or commercio) sets the minimum, the notice period and the holiday entitlement, and it is a reasonable thing to ask about.

Interview questions this chapter answers

Do you have experience with fatturazione elettronica?

If not, say so and show you understand the shape: FatturaPA XML through the SDI, a per-invoice state machine of outcomes with receipts, re-sendable rejections needing idempotency, gapless per-year numbering — which is a genuinely interesting concurrency problem because an identity column leaves gaps on rollback — and ten-year retention that has to be reconciled with GDPR erasure. That answer is worth far more than a vague yes.

How would you support a Power BI reporting project?

Build the model, not the visuals: a star schema with conformed dimensions and a calendar table with working days and Italian holidays, exposed through views so the business rules are decided once. Import mode rather than DirectQuery for most reports, because DirectQuery sends a query per interaction at the production database.

Our product is installed at each customer. What does that change for you?

Every schema change has to be safe on databases I cannot inspect, with very different data volumes and possibly local customisations — so versioned migrations that are re-runnable, backward-compatible expand-and-contract changes, and a lock timeout so a migration fails fast rather than blocking a customer's system. I would also want to know how migrations are currently delivered and rolled back.
